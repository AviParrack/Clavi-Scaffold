# Easter Eggs

*Hidden references, nods, and quiet tributes woven into the workspace and its outputs.*

## What This Is

A collection of easter eggs we can sprinkle across the workspace — in writing, visuals, file names, commit messages, diagrams, and anywhere else something small and meaningful can hide. These are for the people who notice. They reward attention, create texture, and connect our work to the lineages it draws from.

## How to Use

When producing outputs (papers, diagrams, visualizations, commit series, folder names, slide decks), look for natural places to embed references from this collection. They should feel organic — never forced, never explained. If you have to point it out, it's not an easter egg.

**Placement principles:**
- Fit the context. A space paper gets space references; a governance piece gets governance references.
- Vary density. One or two per major output is plenty. More dilutes the effect.
- Layer depth. Some should be obvious to anyone in the field. Some should reward only deep knowledge.

## The Collection

### L5

**Source:** The [L5 Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L5_Society) — founded 1975 by Carolyn and Keith Henson, inspired by Gerard O'Neill's space colonization work. Named after the L5 Lagrange point, where O'Neill proposed building space habitats. Their motto: "L5 in '95!" The society merged with the National Space Institute in 1987 to form the National Space Society.

**Why it matters:** L5 represents the optimistic, citizen-driven vision of space settlement — ordinary people organizing around an extraordinary future. It's the spiritual ancestor of everything in our Space-Energy and SDC work. The L5 point itself is a natural stable equilibrium — things placed there tend to stay.

**Assets:** L5 Society logo (find/reference as needed). The "L5" designation itself. The Lagrange point geometry.

**Placement ideas:**
- Use "L5" as a designation, label, or version name in space-related outputs
- Reference the L5 point in diagrams of cislunar architecture
- The motto "L5 in '95" as a nod to ambitious timelines (echoes our own)
- Logo as a subtle watermark or icon in space presentations
